## Passage

> **17** When Ahab saw Elijah, Ahab said to him, “Is that you, you troubler of Israel?” **18** He answered, “I have not troubled Israel; but you, and your father’s house, in that you have forsaken Yahweh’s commandments, and you have followed the Baals. **19** Now therefore send, and gather to me all Israel to Mount Carmel, and four hundred fifty of the prophets of Baal, and four hundred of the prophets of the Asherah, who eat at Jezebel’s table.” **20** So Ahab sent to all the children of Israel, and gathered the prophets together to Mount Carmel. **21** Elijah came near to all the people, and said, “How long will you waver between the two sides? If Yahweh is God, follow him; but if Baal, then follow him.” The people didn’t say a word. **22** Then Elijah said to the people, “I, even I only, am left as a prophet of Yahweh; but Baal’s prophets are four hundred fifty men. **23** Let them therefore give us two bulls; and let them choose one bull for themselves, and cut it in pieces, and lay it on the wood, and put no fire under; and I will dress the other bull, and lay it on the wood, and put no fire under it. **24** You call on the name of your god, and I will call on Yahweh’s name. The God who answers by fire, let him be God.” All the people answered, “What you say is good.”

> **25** Elijah said to the prophets of Baal, “Choose one bull for yourselves, and dress it first; for you are many; and call on the name of your god, but put no fire under it.” **26** They took the bull which was given them, and they dressed it, and called on the name of Baal from morning even until noon, saying, “Baal, hear us!” But there was no voice, and nobody answered. They leaped about the altar which was made. **27** At noon, Elijah mocked them, and said, “Cry aloud; for he is a god. Either he is deep in thought, or he has gone somewhere, or he is on a journey, or perhaps he sleeps and must be awakened.” **28** They cried aloud, and cut themselves in their way with knives and lances, until the blood gushed out on them. **29** When midday was past, they prophesied until the time of the evening offering; but there was no voice, no answer, and nobody paid attention. **30** Elijah said to all the people, “Come near to me!”; and all the people came near to him. He repaired Yahweh’s altar that had been thrown down. **31** Elijah took twelve stones, according to the number of the tribes of the sons of Jacob, to whom Yahweh’s word came, saying, “Israel shall be your name.” **32** With the stones he built an altar in Yahweh’s name. He made a trench around the altar, large enough to contain two seahs of seed. **33** He put the wood in order, and cut the bull in pieces, and laid it on the wood. He said, “Fill four jars with water, and pour it on the burnt offering, and on the wood.” **34** He said, “Do it a second time”; and they did it the second time. He said, “Do it a third time”; and they did it the third time. **35** The water ran around the altar; and he also filled the trench with water. **36** At the time of the evening offering, Elijah the prophet came near, and said, “Yahweh, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Israel, let it be known today that you are God in Israel, and that I am your servant, and that I have done all these things at your word. **37** Hear me, Yahweh, hear me, that this people may know that you, Yahweh, are God, and that you have turned their heart back again.” **38** Then Yahweh’s fire fell, and consumed the burnt offering, the wood, the stones, and the dust, and licked up the water that was in the trench. **39** When all the people saw it, they fell on their faces. They said, “Yahweh, he is God! Yahweh, he is God!” **40** Elijah said to them, “Seize the prophets of Baal! Don’t let one of them escape!” They seized them; and Elijah brought them down to the brook Kishon, and killed them there.

> **41** Elijah said to Ahab, “Get up, eat and drink; for there is the sound of abundance of rain.” **42** So Ahab went up to eat and to drink. Elijah went up to the top of Carmel; and he bowed himself down on the earth, and put his face between his knees. **43** He said to his servant, “Go up now, and look toward the sea.” He went up, and looked, and said, “There is nothing.” He said, “Go again” seven times. **44** On the seventh time, he said, “Behold, a small cloud, like a man’s hand, is rising out of the sea.” He said, “Go up, tell Ahab, ‘Get ready and go down, so that the rain doesn’t stop you.’” **45** In a little while, the sky grew black with clouds and wind, and there was a great rain. Ahab rode, and went to Jezreel. **46** Yahweh’s hand was on Elijah; and he tucked his cloak into his belt and ran before Ahab to the entrance of Jezreel.

## The Story

1 Kings 18 is Elijah on Mount Carmel: one prophet, 450 prophets of Baal, and the god who answers by fire.

Israel has had three years of drought because Ahab and Jezebel turned the court to Baal. Elijah has been hiding, fed by ravens and then by a widow. That is a different story (1 Kings 17). This chapter is the public contest. Ahab meets him and calls him the troubler of Israel. Elijah throws it back: you forsook Yahweh. Then he names the mountain.

Carmel looks over the sea. Elijah gathers Israel and the 450. The 400 Asherah prophets who eat at Jezebel’s table are invited; the text does not say they show. Elijah’s question is the whole chapter: how long will you limp between two opinions? If Yahweh is God, follow him. If Baal, follow him. The people say nothing.

The test is simple. Two bulls. No fire. The god who answers by fire is God. Baal’s men go first, all morning. They shout. They limp around the altar. Noon: Elijah mocks — maybe Baal is thinking, traveling, or asleep. They cut themselves until the blood runs. Evening: no voice, no answer, nobody paying attention.

Elijah rebuilds the wrecked altar of Yahweh with twelve stones — all Israel, not just the north. He soaks the offering three times until the trench is a moat. His prayer is short. He does not put his head between his knees here. That comes later, for the rain. Fire falls, eats the bull, the wood, the stones, the dust, and the water. The crowd hits the dirt: Yahweh, he is God.

Elijah has the Baal prophets seized and killed at the Kishon. Then he tells Ahab to eat because rain is coming. He climbs Carmel, puts his face between his knees, and sends a servant to look at the sea seven times. A cloud the size of a man’s hand. Then a black sky and a flood. Elijah runs ahead of Ahab’s chariot to Jezreel.

That is why “1 Kings 18” should sit on this page, not on the ravens. The ravens are chapter 17. Carmel is the fire and the rain.

## What It Means

**The drought was already the verdict.** Carmel is God making the verdict public. Baal was a storm god. He cannot light a wet altar or open the sky.

**Limping is the sin the chapter names.** The people will not pick. Elijah will not let them keep both.

**Fire first, then rain.** The people confess. Then the land drinks. The order matters: who is God, then the gift.

**The head between the knees is prayer for rain, not the fire.** 1 Kings 18:42 is after the prophets of Baal are dead. Do not move that posture to the altar scene.

## Questions

### What is the meaning of 1 Kings 18?

1 Kings 18 means Yahweh, not Baal, is God in Israel. Elijah calls a contest on Mount Carmel. Baal’s prophets get silence. Yahweh answers with fire, the people confess, the false prophets die, and the three-year drought ends in rain.

### What happened on Mount Carmel?

Elijah faced 450 prophets of Baal. Two altars, two bulls, no torch. The god who answered by fire would be God. Fire fell on Elijah’s soaked offering. The crowd said, “Yahweh, he is God.” The Baal prophets were killed at the Kishon, and rain returned (1 Kings 18).

### What is the lesson of 1 Kings 18:20-40?

Stop wavering. The lesson of 18:20-40 is that a god who cannot answer is not God, and that Israel’s silence was already a choice. The fire forces the sentence they would not say.

### Why did Elijah put his head between his knees?

After the fire, Elijah climbed Carmel to pray for rain (1 Kings 18:42-44). He bowed to the ground and put his face between his knees while a servant looked toward the sea seven times. It is the posture of the rain prayer, not the fire prayer.

### Is 1 Kings 18 the story of Elijah and the ravens?

No. Ravens feed Elijah in 1 Kings 17:2-6. Mount Carmel is 1 Kings 18. They are consecutive chapters about the same drought. They are not the same scene.
