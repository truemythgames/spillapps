## Passage

> **19** “Now there was a certain rich man, and he was clothed in purple and fine linen, living in luxury every day. **20** A certain beggar, named Lazarus, was taken to his gate, full of sores, **21** and desiring to be fed with the crumbs that fell from the rich man’s table. Yes, even the dogs came and licked his sores. **22** The beggar died, and he was carried away by the angels to Abraham’s bosom. The rich man also died, and was buried. **23** In Hades, he lifted up his eyes, being in torment, and saw Abraham far off, and Lazarus at his bosom. **24** He cried and said, ‘Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water, and cool my tongue! For I am in anguish in this flame.’ **25** “But Abraham said, ‘Son, remember that you, in your lifetime, received your good things, and Lazarus, in the same way, bad things. But here he is now comforted, and you are in anguish. **26** Besides all this, between us and you there is a great gulf fixed, that those who want to pass from here to you are not able, and that no one may cross over from there to us.’ **27** “He said, ‘I ask you therefore, father, that you would send him to my father’s house; **28** for I have five brothers, that he may testify to them, so they won’t also come into this place of torment.’ **29** “But Abraham said to him, ‘They have Moses and the prophets. Let them listen to them.’ **30** “He said, ‘No, father Abraham, but if one goes to them from the dead, they will repent.’ **31** “He said to him, ‘If they don’t listen to Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded if one rises from the dead.’”

## The Story

The rich man and Lazarus is a parable Jesus tells after a run of money talk in Luke 16.

A man lives in purple and fine linen — the clothes of wealth — and feasts every day. At his gate lies Lazarus, sores on his body, wanting crumbs. Dogs lick the sores. In the whole parable, the beggar is the one with a name. The rich man is just “a certain rich man.”

Both die. Angels carry Lazarus to Abraham’s side. The rich man is buried, then lifts his eyes in Hades, in torment, and sees Abraham far off with Lazarus. He still talks like a man with staff. Send Lazarus with a drop of water. Abraham calls him “son” and refuses. The lifetime accounts have swapped. There is a gulf no one can cross.

Then the rich man thinks of his five brothers. Send Lazarus to warn them. Abraham: they have Moses and the prophets. Let them listen. The rich man wants a ghost story — someone from the dead. Abraham’s last line is the knife: if they will not hear Scripture, they will not hear a resurrection either.

Luke’s first readers already know someone has risen from the dead. The parable is aimed at people who wanted a sign more than they wanted the books they already owned. It is also aimed at a gated life that can walk past the same body every day and call that normal.

## What It Means

**The gate was the test, and he failed it daily.** Lazarus was not hidden. He was furniture at the entrance. The sin is not being rich. It is luxury with a dying man in the driveway.

**Death does not start the conversation. It ends the chance to change sides.** The gulf is fixed. The rich man’s first prayer is still to use Lazarus as a servant.

**Moses and the prophets were enough.** The brothers do not need a special appearance. They need to open the books that already tell them what to do with the poor at the gate.

**A name on the beggar and none on the rich man is not an accident.** Jesus gives the ignored man a name that means “God helps.” The man in purple never gets one.

## Questions

### What is the meaning of the parable of Lazarus and the rich man?

That wealth which ignores the person at your gate is judged, that the next life cannot be bargained back, and that Scripture is already the warning. Luke 16:19-31 is not a map of every room in the afterlife. It is a verdict on a gated life and on people who want a miracle instead of Moses.

### Was the rich man and Lazarus a real story?

Jesus tells it as a parable in a chapter of parables. Lazarus is named, which is unusual, so some readers treat it as history. Either way, Jesus is teaching, not writing a travel guide to Hades. The point holds if the two men are characters: the gate, the gulf, and Moses.

### What happened to the rich man and Lazarus after they died?

Lazarus is carried to Abraham’s side and comforted. The rich man is in torment, can see Lazarus, cannot cross, and cannot get a messenger sent to his brothers (Luke 16:22-31).

### What do Christians learn from the parable of the rich man and Lazarus?

That the poor person you already see is the exam. That death locks the choice. That if the Bible does not move you, a spectacular sign will not either. Luke places it next to “you cannot serve God and money” (Luke 16:13) on purpose.

### Is this the same Lazarus Jesus raised?

No. The Lazarus Jesus raises is Mary and Martha’s brother in John 11, in Bethany. This Lazarus is a character in a parable. The shared name is why people mix them. John 11 is a resurrection. Luke 16 is a story about two deaths.
