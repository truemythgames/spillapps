## Passage

> **22** Immediately Jesus made the disciples get into the boat, and to go ahead of him to the other side, while he sent the multitudes away. **23** After he had sent the multitudes away, he went up into the mountain by himself to pray. When evening had come, he was there alone. **24** But the boat was now in the middle of the sea, distressed by the waves, for the wind was contrary. **25** In the fourth watch of the night, Jesus came to them, walking on the sea. **26** When the disciples saw him walking on the sea, they were troubled, saying, “It’s a ghost!” and they cried out for fear. **27** But immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying, “Cheer up! It is I! Don’t be afraid.” **28** Peter answered him and said, “Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the waters.” **29** He said, “Come!” Peter stepped down from the boat, and walked on the waters to come to Jesus. **30** But when he saw that the wind was strong, he was afraid, and beginning to sink, he cried out, saying, “Lord, save me!” **31** Immediately Jesus stretched out his hand, took hold of him, and said to him, “You of little faith, why did you doubt?” **32** When they got up into the boat, the wind ceased. **33** Those who were in the boat came and worshiped him, saying, “You are truly the Son of God!”

## The Story

Jesus walks on water right after he feeds the five thousand.

He makes the disciples get in the boat and go first. He dismisses the crowd. Then he goes up the mountain alone to pray. Matthew wants that solitude in the picture: the miracle on the lake starts with Jesus away from everyone, talking to the Father.

The boat is in the middle of the Sea of Galilee, beaten by waves, wind against it. The “fourth watch” is the last stretch of night, roughly 3 to 6 a.m. They have been rowing for hours. Then they see a figure on the water and decide it is a ghost. Seasoned fishermen, screaming.

Jesus’ first words are not an explanation of physics. “Cheer up! It is I! Don’t be afraid.” “It is I” is *egō eimi* — the same “I am” shape that keeps showing up when he names himself.

Peter does the Peter thing. If it is you, tell me to come. Jesus says come. Peter gets out and walks. Matthew is the only Gospel that includes this part. For a few steps it works. Then Peter sees the wind, gets afraid, and starts to go under. His prayer is three words: “Lord, save me.” Jesus’ hand is immediate. The rebuke is quiet: little faith, why did you doubt? They climb in. The wind stops. The men in the boat worship him: “You are truly the Son of God.”

Mark and John tell the walking without Peter’s step (Mark 6:45-52; John 6:16-21). John adds that the boat reaches land “immediately.” Job 9:8 had already said God “treads on the waves of the sea.” The disciples know that line. That is why they worship instead of asking for a weather report.

## What It Means

**The water is not the point. Who is on it is.** In the Old Testament, the sea is chaos, and only God walks on it. The men in the boat land on the confession: Son of God.

**Fear comes first, then the voice.** They see him and panic. He speaks before he gets in. The miracle is timed to the hour they have no strength left.

**Peter walks until he looks at the storm.** The text does not mock him for getting out. It records that he did walk, then sank when the wind filled his eyes. The rescue is faster than the lecture.

**Worship is the ending, not applause.** When the wind dies they do not high-five the trick. They worship. Matthew is training you to read the lake the same way.

## Questions

### What is the full story of Jesus walking on water?

After feeding the five thousand, Jesus sends the disciples across the Sea of Galilee and goes up the mountain to pray. In the last watch of the night he comes to their storm-beaten boat walking on the sea. They think he is a ghost. He says, “It is I.” Peter asks to come, walks, sinks, and is pulled up. They get in. The wind stops. They worship him as the Son of God (Matthew 14:22-33).

### What is the main message of Jesus walking on water?

That Jesus does what only God does, and that he comes to people who are already exhausted. The story is not a lesson in positive thinking. It is a claim about who is in the boat: the one Job said treads on the sea.

### What does Jesus walk on water mean in Matthew 14:22-33?

Matthew 14:22-33 means Jesus has authority over the sea, that his “I am” is enough to still fear, and that faith is looking at him rather than the wind. Peter’s sink-and-rescue is the human half of the same point: the moment he looks at the storm, he goes under; the moment he cries out, Jesus’ hand is there.

### Who walked on water with Jesus?

Peter. He is the only disciple who gets out of the boat. He walks, then sinks. Jesus catches him. The others stay in the boat and worship when the two of them climb back in.

### Did Jesus walk on the Sea of Galilee?

Yes. Matthew, Mark, and John all place it on the lake the Gospels call the sea — the Sea of Galilee — on the night after the feeding of the five thousand, while the boat was fighting a contrary wind.
