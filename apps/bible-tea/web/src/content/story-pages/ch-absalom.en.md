## Passage

> **9** Absalom happened to meet David's servants. Absalom was riding on his mule, and the mule went under the thick boughs of a great oak, and his head caught hold of the oak, and he was taken up between the sky and earth; and the mule that was under him went on. **10** A certain man saw it, and told Joab, and said, "Behold, I saw Absalom hanging in an oak." **11** Joab said to the man who told him, "Behold, you saw it, and why didn't you strike him there to the ground? I would have given you ten pieces of silver, and a sash." **12** The man said to Joab, "Though I should receive a thousand pieces of silver in my hand, I still wouldn't stretch out my hand against the king's son; for in our hearing the king commanded you and Abishai and Ittai, saying, 'Beware that no one touch the young man Absalom.' **13** Otherwise if I had dealt falsely against his life (and there is no matter hidden from the king), then you yourself would have set yourself against me." **14** Then Joab said, "I'm not going to wait like this with you." He took three darts in his hand, and thrust them through the heart of Absalom, while he was yet alive in the middle of the oak. **15** Ten young men who bore Joab's armor surrounded and struck Absalom, and killed him.

> **31** Behold, the Cushite came. The Cushite said, "News for my lord the king, for Yahweh has avenged you today of all those who rose up against you." **32** The king said to the Cushite, "Is it well with the young man Absalom?" The Cushite answered, "May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who rise up against you to do you harm, be as that young man is." **33** The king was much moved, and went up to the room over the gate, and wept. As he went, he said, "My son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! I wish I had died for you, Absalom, my son, my son!"

## The Story

Absalom was the third son of King David, the most handsome man in Israel, and the one who very nearly took his father's throne. His story runs through 2 Samuel 13 to 19, and it is the story of how David's family came apart.

His mother was Maacah, a princess of Geshur, a small kingdom northeast of Israel. Absalom grew up in a royal household where David had many wives and many sons, and where the king who had been fearless on the battlefield was strangely passive at home. The text describes Absalom's looks in detail: "from the sole of his foot even to the crown of his head there was no blemish in him," and he cut his famously heavy hair once a year and weighed it (2 Samuel 14:25-26).

The trouble started with his half-brother Amnon, David's firstborn. Amnon raped Absalom's full sister Tamar and then threw her out. David was furious but did nothing. Absalom said nothing either. He waited two years, invited all the king's sons to a feast at sheep-shearing time, and had his servants kill Amnon when he was drunk. Then he fled to his grandfather's kingdom in Geshur and stayed there three years.

David mourned Amnon and, the text says, "longed for Absalom," but would not send for him. Joab, David's general, engineered his return with a parable told by a wise woman. Even then David refused to see him. Absalom lived in Jerusalem for two more years without being admitted to his father's presence, until he set Joab's barley field on fire to force a meeting. David finally kissed him. It was too late.

Absalom began to work the crowds. He got a chariot and horses and fifty men to run ahead of him. He stood at the city gate early every morning, intercepted people coming to the king for justice, told each one his case was good but there was no one appointed to hear it, and added that if he were judge, everyone would get justice. He embraced and kissed the people who came to bow to him. "So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel" (2 Samuel 15:6).

After four years he went to Hebron, David's old capital, under the pretext of fulfilling a vow, and had himself proclaimed king. Ahithophel, David's most respected counselor, joined him. The revolt was so broad that David abandoned Jerusalem and fled across the Jordan with his household and his loyal soldiers, weeping as he climbed the Mount of Olives barefoot. Absalom entered the capital and, on Ahithophel's advice, publicly slept with his father's concubines on the palace roof, to show that the break was total.

It ended in the forest of Ephraim. David's veterans destroyed Absalom's larger army in the woods. Absalom, riding a mule, got his head caught in the branches of a great oak and was left hanging while the mule walked on. David had ordered his commanders to spare him. Joab, who had done more than anyone to bring Absalom back, drove three javelins into his chest. His men finished the job, threw the body into a pit, and heaped stones over it.

When the messenger arrived, David asked only one question: "Is it well with the young man Absalom?" Then he went up to the room over the gate and wept, and the whole victory turned to mourning: "My son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! I wish I had died for you." Joab had to rebuke him publicly before he would come out and face the troops who had saved his life.

## What It Means

**Sin in a family compounds when no one deals with it.** David's own adultery with Bathsheba and the murder of her husband came a few chapters earlier, and Nathan the prophet had warned him that the sword would not leave his house. Amnon's crime went unpunished; Absalom's revenge went unpunished; Absalom's return went unreconciled. Every silence made the next disaster larger.

**Absalom wanted his father's throne, not his father.** The text never records him seeking David out. He wanted to be seen, admired, and obeyed, and he was willing to humiliate his father in public to get it. His hair, his chariot, his fifty runners, and the monument he built to himself (2 Samuel 18:18) all point the same way.

**David's grief is the truest thing in the story.** He wept for the son who had tried to kill him, and wished he could have died in his place. Christians have long heard in that cry an echo of a father who did give his son's life for rebels. In Absalom's case the wish could not be granted; in the gospel, it was.

**Charm is not character.** Absalom was the most attractive man in Israel and the people loved him. He was also a murderer who manipulated grievances he had no intention of fixing. The Bible tells his story in full so that readers learn to look past the hair.

## Questions

### Who was Absalom in the Bible?

Absalom was the third son of King David, born to Maacah, daughter of the king of Geshur. He is known for killing his half-brother Amnon to avenge the rape of his sister Tamar, for his exceptional good looks and long hair, and for leading a rebellion that briefly drove David from Jerusalem. He died in the battle of the forest of Ephraim when Joab killed him as he hung by his hair from an oak tree (2 Samuel 13-18).

### Why did Absalom turn against David?

The Bible gives a chain of causes. David failed to punish Amnon for raping Tamar, so Absalom took revenge himself and had to flee. When he returned, David refused to see him for two years. Absalom then spent four years cultivating public resentment against the king's administration and presenting himself as the one who would give people justice. Underneath it all, the text portrays a man who wanted his father's power and had stopped wanting his father.

### What was the sin of Absalom?

Absalom murdered his half-brother Amnon, led an armed rebellion against the king God had anointed, and publicly slept with his father's concubines to declare his claim on the throne (2 Samuel 16:21-22). He also manipulated the people by pretending to care about their legal cases in order to win their loyalty. The Bible treats his rebellion as both a political crime and a sin against God's chosen king.

### How did Absalom die?

During the battle in the forest of Ephraim, Absalom was riding a mule when his head caught in the thick branches of a large oak tree and he was left hanging in the air. David had ordered his commanders to spare him, but Joab, David's general, thrust three javelins into his chest, and ten of Joab's men then struck him and killed him. They threw his body into a pit in the forest and covered it with a great heap of stones (2 Samuel 18:9-17).

### Why did David cry when Absalom died?

Because Absalom was his son, and David loved him despite everything. When the messengers arrived, David's only question was whether Absalom was safe, and when he heard the answer he went to the room above the gate and wept, saying, "My son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! I wish I had died for you" (2 Samuel 18:33). The victory over the rebellion meant nothing to him beside the loss of his child.

### Which wife of David was Absalom's mother?

Maacah, the daughter of Talmai, king of Geshur, a small Aramean kingdom northeast of the Sea of Galilee (2 Samuel 3:3). She was also the mother of Tamar. Absalom fled to his grandfather Talmai in Geshur after killing Amnon and stayed there for three years (2 Samuel 13:37-38).
