## Passage

> **35** On that day, when evening had come, he said to them, “Let’s go over to the other side.” **36** Leaving the multitude, they took him with them, even as he was, in the boat. Other small boats were also with him. **37** A big wind storm arose, and the waves beat into the boat, so much that the boat was already filled. **38** He himself was in the stern, asleep on the cushion, and they woke him up, and told him, “Teacher, don’t you care that we are dying?” **39** He awoke, and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Peace! Be still!” The wind ceased, and there was a great calm. **40** He said to them, “Why are you so afraid? How is it that you have no faith?” **41** They were greatly afraid, and said to one another, “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?”

## The Story

Jesus calms the storm on the same day he has been teaching in parables from a boat.

Evening. He says, “Let’s go over to the other side” — east across the Sea of Galilee, toward Gentile country. They take him “even as he was,” still in the boat he taught from. Other small boats come along. Then a squall hits. Galilee sits in a bowl; cold air off the hills can turn the lake in minutes. Waves fill the boat. These men fish this water for a living. They think they are dying.

Jesus is asleep in the stern on the cushion. That detail is why the story lands. The storm is loud enough to swamp the boat and not loud enough to wake him. They shake him: “Teacher, don’t you care that we are dying?” Mark lets the accusation stand. They do not ask him to do a miracle. They ask if he cares.

He gets up and rebukes the wind the way he rebukes demons. To the sea: “Peace! Be still!” The Greek is closer to “Be silent. Be muzzled.” The wind stops. A great calm. Then he turns on the crew: why so afraid? How is it you have no faith? Their fear does not shrink. It changes targets. They were afraid of the water. Now they are afraid of the man who told the water to sit.

Matthew and Luke tell the same night (Matthew 8:23-27; Luke 8:22-25). Luke has the same question at the end. Psalm 107:23-30 had already described sailors screaming in a storm and God stilling it to a whisper. Mark’s point is that the person asleep on the cushion is that God.

This is a different night from Jesus walking on water (Matthew 14). There he comes across the waves. Here he is already in the boat, asleep, and the storm obeys a sentence.

## What It Means

**The first question is not “how.” It is “who.”** The disciples do not leave the story with a weather lesson. They leave with “Who then is this?”

**He sleeps because he is not afraid.** Their “don’t you care” assumes that silence equals neglect. The sleep is the opposite: the storm is not a threat to him.

**“Peace, be still” is a command, not a prayer.** He does not ask the Father to calm it. He talks to the sea. That is why they are more afraid after the calm than during the waves.

**Faith here is trusting the one in the boat, not the lake staying flat.** He calls out their fear after the miracle, not before. The rebuke is that they decided they were dying while he was in the stern.

## Questions

### What is the Bible verse where Jesus calms the storm?

Mark 4:35-41 is the tightest telling. Jesus says “Peace! Be still!” in verse 39. Matthew 8:23-27 and Luke 8:22-25 are the same event. Do not confuse it with Matthew 14:22-33, where he walks on the water on a later night.

### What is the meaning of the story Jesus calms the storm?

The meaning is that Jesus has God’s authority over chaos, and that his presence in the boat is the answer to “don’t you care.” The storm stopping proves the first. The sleep and the rebuke prove the second.

### What lessons can we learn from Jesus calming the storm?

That fear and faith can sit in the same boat. That you can know Jesus well enough to wake him and still not know who he is. That the word which stills the sea is the same kind of word that stills a man. Mark is not promising you will never take on water. He is showing you who is in the stern.

### Did Jesus calm a storm in Luke 8:22-25?

Yes. Luke 8:22-25 is the same crossing as Mark 4:35-41. Jesus falls asleep, a squall comes down, the boat fills, the disciples wake him, he rebukes wind and water, and they ask who he is that the winds and water obey him.

### What did Jesus say to the storm?

In Mark 4:39 he rebuked the wind and said to the sea, “Peace! Be still!” The wind ceased, and there was a great calm.
