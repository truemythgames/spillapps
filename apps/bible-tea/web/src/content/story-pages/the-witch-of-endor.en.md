## Passage

> **3** Now Samuel was dead, and all Israel had mourned for him, and buried him in Ramah, even in his own city. Saul had sent away those who had familiar spirits and the wizards out of the land. **4** The Philistines gathered themselves together, and came and encamped in Shunem; and Saul gathered all Israel together, and they encamped in Gilboa. **5** When Saul saw the army of the Philistines, he was afraid, and his heart trembled greatly. **6** When Saul inquired of Yahweh, Yahweh didn't answer him by dreams, by Urim, or by prophets.
>
> **7** Then Saul said to his servants, "Seek for me a woman who has a familiar spirit, that I may go to her, and inquire of her." His servants said to him, "Behold, there is a woman who has a familiar spirit at Endor." **8** Saul disguised himself and put on other clothing, and went, he and two men with him, and they came to the woman by night. Then he said, "Please consult for me by the familiar spirit, and bring me up whomever I shall name to you." **9** The woman said to him, "Behold, you know what Saul has done, how he has cut off those who have familiar spirits, and the wizards, out of the land. Why then do you lay a snare for my life, to cause me to die?" **10** Saul swore to her by Yahweh, saying, "As Yahweh lives, no punishment will happen to you for this thing." **11** Then the woman said, "Whom shall I bring up to you?" He said, "Bring Samuel up for me."
>
> **12** When the woman saw Samuel, she cried with a loud voice; and the woman spoke to Saul, saying, "Why have you deceived me? For you are Saul!" **13** The king said to her, "Don't be afraid! What do you see?" The woman said to Saul, "I see a god coming up out of the earth." **14** He said to her, "What does he look like?" She said, "An old man comes up. He is covered with a robe." Saul perceived that it was Samuel, and he bowed with his face to the ground, and showed respect.
>
> **15** Samuel said to Saul, "Why have you disturbed me, to bring me up?" Saul answered, "I am very distressed; for the Philistines make war against me, and God has departed from me, and answers me no more, by prophets, or by dreams. Therefore I have called you, that you may make known to me what I shall do." **16** Samuel said, "Why then do you ask me, since Yahweh has departed from you and has become your adversary? **17** Yahweh has done to you as he spoke by me. Yahweh has torn the kingdom out of your hand, and given it to your neighbor, even to David. **18** Because you didn't obey Yahweh's voice, and didn't execute his fierce wrath on Amalek, therefore Yahweh has done this thing to you today. **19** Moreover Yahweh will deliver Israel also with you into the hand of the Philistines; and tomorrow you and your sons will be with me. Yahweh will deliver the army of Israel also into the hand of the Philistines."
>
> **20** Then Saul fell immediately his full length on the earth, and was terrified, because of Samuel's words. There was no strength in him; for he had eaten no bread all day long or all night long. **21** The woman came to Saul, and saw that he was very troubled, and said to him, "Behold, your servant has listened to your voice, and I have put my life in my hand, and have listened to your words which you spoke to me. **22** Now therefore, please listen also to the voice of your servant, and let me set a morsel of bread before you. Eat, that you may have strength, when you go on your way." **23** But he refused, and said, "I will not eat." But his servants, together with the woman, constrained him; and he listened to their voice. So he arose from the earth and sat on the bed. **24** The woman had a fattened calf in the house. She hurried and killed it; and she took flour, and kneaded it, and baked unleavened bread of it. **25** She brought it before Saul, and before his servants; and they ate. Then they rose up, and went away that night.

## The Story

The witch of Endor is not really a story about a witch. It is a story about a king who has run out of options.

By 1 Samuel 28, Saul has been Israel's king for decades and has been losing God's favor for most of them. Samuel, the prophet who anointed him, is dead. David, the man Samuel said would replace him, is on the run and gathering strength. And now the Philistine army has camped at Shunem, across the valley from Saul's position on Mount Gilboa, in numbers that make Saul's heart "tremble greatly."

He does the right thing first. He prays. He asks God for direction through the three channels Israel had: dreams, the Urim (the high priest's lot), and prophets. Silence on all three. Saul has spent years ignoring God's voice, and now, when he finally wants it, it is gone.

So he reaches for the one thing he had outlawed. Earlier in his reign Saul had expelled every medium and spiritist from Israel, obeying the law in Deuteronomy 18 that treats necromancy as a capital offense. Now he asks his servants to find him one. They know exactly where to look: a woman at Endor, a village on the far side of the Philistine lines.

Saul goes at night, in disguise, with two men. The woman is wary. She assumes the stranger is a plant, sent to trap her under the very law Saul wrote. Saul swears in God's name that she will not be punished, an oath that is itself absurd: the king invoking the Lord to protect a practice the Lord forbade.

She asks who he wants. "Bring Samuel up for me."

What happens next is the part nobody agrees on. The text says plainly that "the woman saw Samuel" and screamed. Her fright is telling. Whatever she normally did for clients, she did not expect this. The moment she sees the figure, she also sees through the disguise: "You are Saul!" The king tells her not to be afraid and asks what she sees: an old man wrapped in a robe, the prophet's robe Saul once tore in a fit of desperation years earlier. Saul bows to the ground.

Samuel's first words are a complaint: "Why have you disturbed me?" Saul pours out his fear. Samuel's reply is the message Saul has been trying not to hear for years, now delivered with no softening. God has left you. He has become your adversary. The kingdom has already gone to David. This happened because you disobeyed at Amalek. And tomorrow you and your sons will die, and Israel will fall to the Philistines.

Saul collapses full length on the floor. He has not eaten in a day and a night. The woman, whom the story has set up as the villain, is the one who kneels beside him. She kills the fattened calf she was keeping, bakes bread, and feeds the king who had once condemned her. He eats, gets up, and leaves in the dark.

The next chapters record that everything Samuel said came true. Saul's three sons, including Jonathan, die on Gilboa. Saul, wounded, falls on his own sword.

## What It Means

The story is placed deliberately. Chapter 28 opens with David, Saul's replacement, being pressed into the Philistine army (1 Samuel 28:1-2), and it sits three chapters before the battle where Saul dies (1 Samuel 31). It is the last night of a king, and the writer wants you to watch how he spends it.

**Saul's problem was never a lack of information.** He knew what God wanted. Samuel had told him in person, years earlier, when he spared Amalek's king and livestock against explicit orders (1 Samuel 15). The kingdom was declared lost that day. Everything after is Saul refusing to accept a verdict he already heard. At Endor he does not learn anything new. He gets the same message, from the same prophet, one last time.

**The silence was a consequence, not a mystery.** "Yahweh didn't answer him by dreams, by Urim, or by prophets." Saul had killed the priests at Nob (1 Samuel 22), chased away the prophets' friend David, and treated Samuel's counsel as optional. The channels went quiet because he had spent years cutting them.

**Forbidden shortcuts still lead somewhere.** Whatever you conclude about the apparition, the point of the passage is that Saul's rebellion had a final form: seeking from the dead what he refused to accept from the living God. Chronicles names this act specifically as one of the reasons for his death (1 Chronicles 10:13).

**Grace shows up in the least expected person.** The medium is the only character in the chapter who shows Saul kindness. The law-breaker feeds the law-giver. The Bible does not endorse her trade, but it records her compassion, and lets the contrast stand.

## Questions

### Who was the witch of Endor in the Bible?

The witch of Endor was a medium, a woman who claimed to consult the dead, living in the village of Endor in northern Israel. She is never named. King Saul visited her in secret the night before his final battle, asking her to summon the dead prophet Samuel (1 Samuel 28). She appears only in this one chapter.

### Did the witch of Endor really summon Samuel?

The text says she did: "When the woman saw Samuel, she cried with a loud voice" (1 Samuel 28:12), and the figure speaks a true prophecy that comes to pass the next day. Her scream suggests even she did not expect it. Christian interpreters have long debated whether God permitted Samuel to appear, whether the figure was a demonic impersonation, or whether the medium was a fraud who was caught off guard. The straightforward reading of the passage, and the one held by most early Jewish and Christian commentators, is that God allowed Samuel to appear to deliver Saul's sentence.

### Why did Saul go to the witch of Endor?

Because God had stopped answering him. Facing a massive Philistine army, Saul "inquired of Yahweh" but received nothing by dreams, by Urim, or by prophets (1 Samuel 28:6). Desperate for any word about the coming battle, he sought out a medium, breaking his own law and God's.

### What did Samuel say to Saul at Endor?

Samuel told Saul that God had become his adversary, that the kingdom had already been given to David, that this was the result of Saul's disobedience at Amalek, and that "tomorrow you and your sons will be with me" (1 Samuel 28:16-19). All of it happened the next day at Mount Gilboa.

### Where was Endor?

Endor was a village in the territory of Manasseh, near Mount Tabor in the Jezreel Valley, a few miles north of Saul's camp on Mount Gilboa and behind the Philistine lines at Shunem. Saul had to travel at night, in disguise, through enemy territory to reach it. The name is preserved in the Arab village of Indur, near modern Ein Dor in Israel.

### Is consulting a medium forbidden in the Bible?

Yes. Deuteronomy 18:10-12 forbids consulting mediums, spiritists, and anyone who "consults with the dead," and Leviticus 20:27 makes it a capital offense. Saul himself had enforced this law (1 Samuel 28:3). The story treats his visit to Endor as the final act of a long rebellion, and 1 Chronicles 10:13 names it as a cause of his death.
