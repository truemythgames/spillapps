## Passage

> **19** This is the history of the generations of Isaac, Abraham’s son. Abraham became the father of Isaac. **20** Isaac was forty years old when he took Rebekah, the daughter of Bethuel the Syrian of Paddan Aram, the sister of Laban the Syrian, to be his wife. **21** Isaac entreated Yahweh for his wife, because she was barren. Yahweh was entreated by him, and Rebekah his wife conceived. **22** The children struggled together within her. She said, “If it is so, why do I live?” She went to inquire of Yahweh. **23** Yahweh said to her, “Two nations are in your womb. Two peoples will be separated from your body. The one people will be stronger than the other people. The elder will serve the younger.” **24** When her days to be delivered were fulfilled, behold, there were twins in her womb. **25** The first came out red all over, like a hairy garment. They named him Esau. **26** After that, his brother came out, and his hand had hold on Esau’s heel. He was named Jacob. Isaac was sixty years old when she bore them. **27** The boys grew. Esau was a skillful hunter, a man of the field. Jacob was a quiet man, living in tents. **28** Now Isaac loved Esau, because he ate his venison. Rebekah loved Jacob. **29** Jacob boiled stew. Esau came in from the field, and he was famished. **30** Esau said to Jacob, “Please feed me with that same red stew, for I am famished.” Therefore his name was called Edom. **31** Jacob said, “First, sell me your birthright.” **32** Esau said, “Behold, I am about to die. What good is the birthright to me?” **33** Jacob said, “Swear to me first.” He swore to him. He sold his birthright to Jacob. **34** Jacob gave Esau bread and stew of lentils. He ate and drank, rose up, and went his way. So Esau despised his birthright.

## The Story

Jacob and Esau are twins, and the fight starts before they are born.

Isaac prays because Rebekah cannot conceive. God answers. Then the pregnancy is so violent she asks why she is even alive. God tells her two nations are in her womb, and the older will serve the younger. That sentence hangs over everything that follows.

Esau comes out first, red and hairy. Jacob comes out holding his heel. The names stick: Esau is associated with being hairy; Jacob means something like “he grabs the heel,” which in Hebrew also means he deceives. Isaac is sixty. The boys grow into opposite men. Esau hunts. Jacob stays in the tents. Isaac loves Esau for the meat. Rebekah loves Jacob.

The first theft is a bowl of stew. Esau comes in starving. Jacob will not feed him until Esau sells the birthright — the double portion and the family headship that belong to the firstborn. Esau swears it away, eats, and leaves. Genesis’s verdict is one line: “So Esau despised his birthright.” Hebrews later calls him godless for trading it for a single meal (Hebrews 12:16).

Years later Isaac is old and blind. He wants to bless Esau before he dies. Rebekah overhears and runs a costume change: Esau’s clothes, goat skins on Jacob’s arms and neck, goat meat dressed as venison. Jacob’s only objection is that he might get caught and cursed. Rebekah takes the curse on herself. Jacob walks in and lies three times: “I am Esau your firstborn.” When Isaac asks how the hunt was so fast, Jacob puts God’s name on the lie.

Isaac almost stops it. The voice is Jacob’s. The hands feel like Esau’s. He blesses him anyway: dew, grain, wine, nations bowing, lord over his brothers. Jacob leaves. Esau walks in with the real meal. Isaac trembles. The blessing, once spoken, will not be pulled back. Esau weeps the “exceeding great and bitter cry.” Isaac has a lesser word left: Esau will live by the sword, serve his brother, and one day shake the yoke off.

Esau plans to kill Jacob when the mourning for Isaac is over. Rebekah sends Jacob north to Laban. That flight is the next story — the night he sleeps on a rock and dreams of a stairway. Decades later the brothers meet again, and Esau runs to Jacob and weeps on his neck (Genesis 33). The stolen blessing is not the last word. The last word is a reunion neither of them could have written in chapter 27.

## What It Means

**God chose before the stew.** “The elder will serve the younger” is spoken in the womb (Genesis 25:23). Paul quotes it in Romans 9:10-13 to say the choice was not based on works. That does not clean Jacob’s hands. It means the promise was already on him when he cheated to grab it.

**Birthright and blessing are not the same thing.** The birthright (Genesis 25) is the firstborn’s legal share. The blessing (Genesis 27) is the father’s spoken future. Jacob takes both. Esau treats the first as worthless and then screams when he loses the second.

**The family is split down the middle.** Isaac’s stomach picks Esau. Rebekah’s scheme picks Jacob. Nobody in this chapter is a hero. God still keeps the Abraham promise moving through the liar, not the hunter.

**Esau is not erased.** He becomes a people — Edom — and Isaac’s last words to him come true: he lives by the sword and later breaks free (2 Kings 8:20-22). Genesis 33 shows him rich, armed, and willing to forgive. The story is about the promise line, not about God hating a twin for being hungry.

## Questions

### What is Jacob and Esau's story about?

Jacob and Esau are twin sons of Isaac and Rebekah (Genesis 25 and 27). God tells Rebekah the older will serve the younger. Esau sells Jacob his birthright for stew, then Jacob — dressed as Esau — steals their blind father’s blessing. Esau weeps and plans murder. Jacob flees. Years later they reconcile (Genesis 33). The story is about a chosen line moving through a deceiver, and a family that almost destroys itself over a blessing.

### Why did God choose Jacob over Esau?

Genesis 25:23 says it before they are born: two nations, the older serving the younger. Malachi 1:2-3 and Romans 9:10-13 pick that up as God’s choice, not a reward for Jacob’s later tricks. The text never says Esau was rejected because he was red, or a hunter, or hungry. It says the promise would run through Jacob.

### What color was Esau in the Bible?

Genesis 25:25 says the first twin “came out red all over, like a hairy garment.” That redness is why he is also called Edom, which means “red” (Genesis 25:30), after the red stew. The Bible is describing his appearance at birth, not a modern racial category.

### What happened to Esau in the Bible?

After the stolen blessing, Esau marries, becomes the father of the Edomites, and meets Jacob years later with four hundred men — then embraces him instead of killing him (Genesis 33). His descendants settle in Seir. Isaac’s word that Esau would serve and later break loose plays out in Edom’s later history with Israel.

### Were Jacob and Esau twins?

Yes. Genesis 25:24-26: twins in one womb. Esau first, Jacob second, holding Esau’s heel. Isaac was sixty when they were born.

### Did Jacob and Esau make peace?

Yes. In Genesis 33 Jacob returns from Laban terrified. Esau runs, embraces him, and weeps. They do not share a household after that — Jacob settles elsewhere — but the murder plot is over.
